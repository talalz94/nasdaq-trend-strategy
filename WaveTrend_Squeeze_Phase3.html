36 signal/threshold/stop configs × every S&P 500 name × 10 years of clean daily data, each scored out-of-sample (train 2016–21 / test 2022–26), then sliced by regime, sector, weekday, volatility and stock. The sweep hands you spectacular per-trade numbers — +211 bps out-of-sample! — but stress-tested against market drift, that edge evaporates. What actually survives is subtler and more useful: a regime playbook and a materially smoother ride.

01

The drift trap — why the "winner" isn't one

The sweep's champion (MA-Shift + trend filter) shows +304 bps per trade. But it holds ~48 days, and over any random 48-day window in this decade the market returned +308 bps. Subtract the drift and the timing edge is −4 bps — essentially zero. The big number was market beta wearing a signal's costume. On daily holds, longer = bigger per-trade P&L purely from more exposure (the weekly "1,163 bps" in §6 is the same illusion, amplified).

The honest verdict

On a raw-return basis the strategies trail buy & hold: champion CAGR 11.3%, regime-combo 14.6%, vs equal-weight buy & hold 15.8%. A long-only signal in a decade-long bull market mostly re-discovers the bull market.

This is exactly why a sweep needs a drift benchmark — rank by raw P&L and you'll "optimise" your way into leveraged beta and call it alpha.

So where's the real timing edge?

At intraday timeframes. On 1h bars (phase 1–2) the oversold-bounce held +36 bps over matched drift — because a ~10-hour hold has almost zero drift, so the signal's timing carries the result. Stretch the hold to weeks and drift drowns the signal. Edge lives on short holds; beta lives on long ones.

02

What actually survives: the regime playbook

The durable finding isn't one magic setting — it's that different signals own different regimes (market vs its 200-day SMA + volatility → BULL / BEAR / CHOPPY). Each cell below is the best signal for that regime, scored on out-of-sample test-period trades. This is a real, data-driven playbook.

Read the regimes right

Bull → trend-follow (buy MA-Shift pullbacks in uptrends). Bear → buy capitulation (SQZ-colour oversold- momentum bounces, PF 4.7). Choppy → fade extremes (WaveTrend deep-oversold −70 mean-reversion). Caveat: clean BEAR days are rare (~1% of the decade), so the bear cell rests on a thin sample — directionally clear, statistically light.

03

The payoff is risk-adjusted, not raw

Switching signals by regime doesn't beat buy & hold on return — but it delivers a far better ride: higher Sharpe, shallower drawdowns, and it sits partly in cash. For anyone using leverage or who can't stomach a −35% index drawdown, that's the whole game.

06

Timeframe — bigger numbers, same beta

Resampling daily → weekly triples-to-quadruples the per-trade P&L (MA-Shift: 304→1,163 bps, PF 1.9→3.7). Tempting — but it's the drift trap again: weekly trades hold many weeks, so they simply bank more of the bull market. Higher timeframe ≠ more edge; it's more exposure. Genuine timing edge moves the other way — down to intraday.

08

The honest "best settings"

There isn't one number — it depends on what you're optimising for:

  • Max raw return? Just buy & hold the index (15.8% CAGR). No daily signal here beats it — they capture the same beta with less exposure.
  • Best risk-adjusted ride? The regime-switched combo — bull: MA-Shift trend-pullback · bear: SQZ-colour bounce · choppy: WaveTrend −70. Sharpe 1.93 (vs ~0.8 for equities), max DD −16%, test-period CAGR 12.8% at Sharpe 1.44.
  • Real timing alpha? Go intraday. The 1h oversold-bounce (phase 1–2) is the only thing that beat matched drift, and it's asset-specific (works on mean-reverting equities, fails on trending gold).
  • Universal rules that held: deeper oversold = higher quality; don't cut winners (stops trade return for safety); align the tactic to the regime; and always benchmark against drift.
09

Caveats

  • Survivorship: 462 of the 498 tickers have full 2016 history, but they're today's index — delisted names that fell out over the decade aren't here, which flatters long-only results. (A point-in-time membership study is the separate follow-up.)
  • Daily only: Alpaca's free hourly is served in ~291-bar pages → 10yr × 500 names ≈ 6–7 hours to pull, so intraday depth stays on the phase-1/2 data. Time-of-day isn't covered here.
  • Drift-inflated absolutes: per-trade bps include market drift; the relative ranking and the risk-adjusted portfolio figures are the trustworthy outputs.
  • Thin BEAR sample: clean bear-regime days are ~1% of the decade — the bear playbook is directional, not statistically heavy.
