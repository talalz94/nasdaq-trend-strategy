01

Same signal, opposite result — stocks vs gold

The single most important finding of phase 2. The WaveTrend oversold-bounce is a mean-reversion bet — it needs an asset that snaps back. Stocks (in a choppy window) do; gold trends, so the same entry gets run over. Momentum signals, by contrast, work on both. There is no universal edge — match the tactic to the asset's character.

Net edge per trade (bps, 1h) — stocks vs gold, by signal
Why this matters

If we'd only ever looked at the 42-day stock sample, we'd have "concluded" oversold-bounce is a great gold strategy too — and lost money live. Cross-asset testing is the cheapest insurance against a false edge.

The filter must match the regime

Stocks (mean-reversion) want a quality / sector veto — a trend filter hurts the dip-buyer. Gold (momentum) wants a trend-alignment veto (price > MA40), which roughly doubles its edge (18→38 bps, PF 1.32→1.81). Same idea, opposite filter.

06

$1,000 + leverage — how much without blowing up?

Constant-leverage compounding of the validated gold champion (single instrument, one position at a time, 3% protective stop, 21 years). Kelly says the growth-optimal leverage is ~9–10× — but that carries a 34–49% chance of a 50% drawdown. "Without blowing up" is a much lower number.

The honest answer

~3× is the "don't blow up" zone: $1,000 → ~$9,700 over 21 years (11% CAGR), −29% max drawdown, ruin risk <3%. 5× (≈half-Kelly) nearly triples it (~$27k) but you must accept ~45% drawdowns and ~1-in-8 ruin. Past ~15× the account almost certainly blows up.

Read the fine print

The giant "95th percentile" figures at high leverage are lottery tails, not expectations. Real ruin risk is higher than modelled: gold gaps on news (the 3% stop can slip), and overnight funding costs on a leveraged CFD aren't included — they'd bite the 1h strategy most. Size like the drawdowns are real, because they are.
