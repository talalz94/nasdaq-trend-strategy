What survived everything:
four simple rules.

Across this project I tested roughly 200 variants — indicators, timeframes, regimes, leverage, options, sectors, geopolitics, seasonality, breadth, sentiment. The overwhelming majority failed. What's left is a strategy of four unglamorous rules that turned $1 into $4,215 since 1988 (24.2%/yr) with a −31% worst drawdown — and, crucially, was positive through both the dot-com bust and the GFC. The single biggest lesson of the whole exercise: static beats clever, and only leading indicators help.

WHY EACH RULE EXISTS
  • Trend gate — leverage must never ride a downtrend. Avoiding the −80% craters is the entire edge.
  • Vol targeting — the single most valuable knob. It is what let leverage survive the dot-com crash.
  • Half-size in chop — full de-levering threw away 8%/yr; leverage still pays in choppy uptrends.
  • Bonds, not cash — treasuries rally in the flight-to-safety moments that trigger risk-off.
  • Gold sleeve — insurance for a stagflationary decade, which bonds cannot hedge.
WHAT DIDN'T MAKE THE CUT
  • SQQQ / shorting — lost in every configuration; inverse leveraged ETFs decay to zero.
  • Momentum rotation across assets — made the dead decade worse (3.7% vs 7.9%).
  • 18 geopolitical adaptations — not one beat the static allocation.
  • VIX, dollar, credit spreads, breadth — all coincident or lagging; they sell the bottom.
  • Boosting leverage in strong trends — literally zero effect.

Crisis by crisis — including where it fails

Total return through each event. The strategy is built to survive slow, deep bear markets — and it does, spectacularly. But it has two real weaknesses, shown honestly below.

Two honest weaknesses

Sharp V-shaped scares (2011 EU crisis: −21.6% while Nasdaq was only −1.5%). A fast drop that recovers immediately whipsaws the trend gate: it de-risks into the hole, then misses the snap-back. Any trend system has this flaw.

Stock-and-bond-down regimes (2022: −21.9%). When rates spike, the treasury off-leg fails at exactly the wrong moment. Gold cushioned it, but did not save it. There is no complete fix — this is the one regime that hurts every component simultaneously.

One redundancy worth knowing

With bonds and gold in place, removing the yield-curve rule changes nothing (Calmar 0.79 either way). It earned its keep in the simpler strategy; in the final one the defensive sleeves already do that job. It is harmless insurance, not a driver.

The meta-lesson in one line

Leading indicators help; coincident ones hurt; and static allocation beats timing almost everywhere. The yield curve worked because it inverts a year before recessions. VIX, the dollar, credit spreads, breadth and every geopolitical momentum flag failed because by the time they fire, the move has happened. Every attempt to time a diversifier destroyed the value that simply holding it provided.

Read this before risking a dollar

  • You have not deployed capital, and nothing here says you should. This is a research result. Paper-trade the weekly scanner for a few months before considering anything else.
  • Expect a −31% drawdown, and plan for worse. The backtest's worst is not the future's worst. If watching $10,000 become $6,900 would make you abandon the rules, size smaller — the strategy only works if followed through the pain.
  • 1988–2026 contained the greatest technology bull market in history. Do not extrapolate 24%/yr. A sober planning figure is roughly half that.
  • Pre-2010 TQQQ is simulated (conservatively) from the index, and gold is proxied by the miners index before 2004 — higher beta than bullion, so the real gold sleeve would be gentler and somewhat less effective.
  • Selection cost is real (0.07–0.12 Calmar measured). Discount the headline figures accordingly.
  • The 2022 regime is the unsolved problem. When stocks and bonds fall together there is no clean hedge in this framework.
  • Leveraged ETFs carry structural risks beyond price: daily-reset decay, borrow costs, and issuer risk. Never hold them unmanaged.
