01

What the two indicators actually do here

Both are LazyBear oscillators, computed to the exact settings in your screenshots (WaveTrend 10/21, OB 60/53, OS −60/−53; Squeeze BB 20×2.0, KC 20×1.5, TrueRange on). The linear-regression momentum was validated to match numpy.polyfit to 6 decimals.

WaveTrend — the timing trigger

A normalised, scale-free oscillator. The crossover of its fast line (wt1) over the signal line (wt2) times entries and exits. Alone it whipsaws and loses money. The magic is the location filter: only take the cross when wt2 is already deep in oversold territory (<−53). That single condition flips a losing crossover into a +41 bps edge.

→ Confirms your idea #2 exactly: in the extreme zones, price bounces — so wait for the turn rather than catching the falling knife.

Squeeze Momentum — the confirmation

Histogram value + a 4-colour state (positive rising / positive falling / negative falling / negative rising). Your idea #3 — "negative but colour turning up = buy" — is real but weak on its own (p=0.18, not significant).

As a filter on the WaveTrend oversold cross, though, it earns its keep: requiring momentum to already be ticking up lifts per-trade return from +41 → +48 bps and win rate to 45%, at the cost of ~half the trades. Quality over quantity.

02

The honest test: edge over drift, not raw return

Raw returns lie. On the daily timeframe the market drifted up +1.06% over any random 12-bar hold in this window — so almost any long strategy "works" on 1d/4h purely from beta. The only metric that isolates the indicators is return minus the unconditional drift at the same holding horizon. Ranked that way, the oversold-bounce family dominates — and it does best where drift is smallest (1h, 4h), which is the tell that it's timing skill, not market luck.

Exit engineering — your scalp idea, tested to destruction

Idea #7 was "take the small profit and get out." I tested it directly against letting the trade run to the next WaveTrend bearish cross. It backfires. The entire edge lives in the tail of the bounce; cutting winners early throws it away. The one risk control that helps is a wide catastrophic stop.

Trailing 1.5% cuts avg return from +41 bps to −0.1 bps. A tight scalp (target 3%/stop 2%) halves it to +20 bps. The full-bounce exit (bearish cross) is the profit engine.

One stop worth keeping

A −2% hard stop barely dents return (48.1→48.1 bps on Confluence) while lifting PF 1.51→1.58 and cutting pooled drawdown ~12%. On 4h it pushes PF to 1.71, Sharpe 0.19. This is the recommended config.

06

Realistic account curve — capital-constrained

Per-trade stats ignore capital. This is an event-driven portfolio: $100k, each signal risks a fixed slice of current equity, capped concurrent positions, closes-before-opens, net of costs. Result over the 42-day window: +7.5% vs +0.76% equal-weight buy-and-hold — ~10× the market with a shallow −3.6% drawdown and annualised Sharpe 3.5.

Equity curve — $100k start · 10 positions · 10% risk each  ·  dashed = buy & hold
Capacity is the real constraint

Only 191 of 850 signals fit into 10 slots — because oversold signals cluster (when the market dips, dozens of names flash oversold at once). Widening to 20 smaller positions banks more of them and lifts Sharpe to 3.73 at half the drawdown. The strategy wants breadth, not concentration.

07

Options — leverage, not a better edge

Idea #6: route the same long signals through ATM weekly calls (Black-Scholes, realised vol as IV, 5-DTE, 1% premium friction). Expectancy jumps from +0.48% to +11.9% per trade — but that's pure delta leverage on the same signal. The profit factor barely moves (1.56 vs 1.58), win rate drops to 35%, the median trade is −22%, and all the money is in a thin right tail (best +497%).

09

Reality check — read this before risking a dollar

The edge is real in this data. Whether it persists live depends on things this 42-day sample can't settle. Honest limitations:

  • Sample is 42 trading days (2026-05-26 → 07-24) — that's all the store holds. Significant, but one regime. The window was choppy/mildly-up (+0.76%, −2.3% mid-drawdown), which flatters mean-reversion. A trending or crashing regime could behave very differently. Treat as promising, not proven.
  • Costs modelled at 2 bps/side (liquid large-caps, commission-free). Realistic, but the edge is only ~4× costs on 1h — slippage on fast fills matters. On 15m the edge (5 bps) barely clears costs; don't trade this on 15m.
  • Entries at next-bar open, no lookahead — but real fills on an oversold spike can gap past the open. Assume some slippage tax.
  • Mild survivorship bias: the universe is current S&P 500 membership. Over 2 months the distortion is small.
  • Options numbers are illustrative: realised vol ≠ implied vol, mid-price fills assumed, no smile/vol-crush. Directionally right, optimistic in level.
  • Per-stock tables are noisy (3–7 trades). Trust the sector and pooled numbers, not individual tickers.
  • No walk-forward / out-of-sample split yet — the natural next step once the store has more history.
